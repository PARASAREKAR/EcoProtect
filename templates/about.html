Fundamentals

What Is the Environment?

The environment encompasses all living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) things that interact as a system on Earth. Scientists organise it into four interconnected spheres, each playing a vital and unique role in sustaining all life.

🌿

Biosphere

The zone of life

The biosphere encompasses all living organisms on Earth and the environments they inhabit — from the deepest ocean trenches to 10 km altitude in the atmosphere. It represents the thin, fragile shell of life on our planet.

🌍 Extent:From −11km (ocean floor) to +10km altitude
🦠 Contains:~8.7 million estimated eukaryotic species
⚠ Threat:Only 3% of Earth's land remains ecologically intact
🌊

Hydrosphere

All of Earth's water

The hydrosphere includes all water on, above, and below Earth's surface — oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater, glaciers, and water vapour. Water covers 71% of Earth's surface and is the primary medium for all biochemical reactions of life.

💧 Volume:1.386 billion km³ of water on Earth
🧊 Fresh:Only 2.5% freshwater; 69% locked in ice
⚠ Crisis:40% of world faces water stress by 2030 — UN
🏔️

Lithosphere

Earth's rocky shell

The lithosphere is the solid, rocky outer shell of Earth — encompassing the crust and upper mantle. It provides the mineral nutrients that form soils, the foundation for terrestrial ecosystems, and the geological cycles that regulate climate over millions of years.

🌱 Soil:95% of all food is produced from soil — FAO
⏱️ Rates:1cm of topsoil takes 1,000 years to form
⚠ Loss:24 billion tonnes of fertile soil lost annually — UN
🌤️

Atmosphere

Earth's protective blanket

The atmosphere is the 480km-deep layer of gases surrounding Earth. It regulates temperature, shields against UV radiation via the ozone layer, drives weather and climate, and provides the oxygen that every animal needs to breathe.

🌬️ Mix:78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 1% other gases
🌡️ CO₂:422 ppm CO₂ in 2024 — highest in 800,000 years
⚠ Change:+1.2°C average warming above pre-industrial — IPCC
Planetary Systems

Earth's Four Key Biogeochemical Cycles

These cycles continuously recycle the essential elements of life — carbon, water, nitrogen and oxygen — through living organisms, soils, oceans and the atmosphere.

🔄

The Carbon Cycle

Regulates Earth's temperature by cycling carbon through the atmosphere, oceans, land & living things.

  1. Photosynthesis: Plants absorb CO₂ from air, store as organic matter
  2. Respiration: Animals & decomposers release CO₂ back to atmosphere
  3. Ocean Exchange: Seas absorb ~25% of anthropogenic CO₂
  4. Human Disruption: Burning fossil fuels adds 37B tonnes/year — IEA
💧

The Water Cycle

Continuously moves water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation and runoff — purifying and distributing freshwater across the globe.

  1. Evaporation: Solar energy converts surface water to vapour
  2. Transpiration: Forests release up to 95% of water absorbed
  3. Condensation: Vapour cools to form clouds and rain
  4. Infiltration: Water soaks into soil, recharging groundwater
🌾

The Nitrogen Cycle

Cycles nitrogen — essential for proteins and DNA — through the atmosphere, soil, water and living organisms. Heavily disrupted by synthetic fertilisers.

  1. Fixation: Bacteria convert atmospheric N₂ into ammonia
  2. Nitrification: Bacteria convert ammonia to nitrates in soil
  3. Uptake: Plants absorb nitrates for growth and protein
  4. Denitrification: Returns nitrogen gas to atmosphere
🫧

The Oxygen Cycle

Maintains Earth's 21% atmospheric oxygen — a remarkable stability maintained by photosynthetic life for 2.5 billion years and sustained by both land plants and marine phytoplankton.

  1. Production: Photosynthesis releases O₂ as a byproduct
  2. Ocean Source: Phytoplankton produce ~50% of Earth's oxygen
  3. Consumption: Respiration & decomposition consume oxygen
  4. Ozone Shield: O₃ layer blocks 97% of UV-B radiation

Economic Reality

Nature's Economy: $125 Trillion Per Year

Costanza et al. (2014) estimated the annual economic value of global ecosystem services — the services nature provides humanity for free. This dwarfs the entire global GDP. We don't pay for it, but we cannot live without it.

🌾
$29T
Provisioning Services

Food, fresh water, timber, fibre, medicinal plants, genetic resources — the direct material goods nature supplies.

🌡️
$37T
Regulating Services

Climate regulation, flood control, disease suppression, water purification, air quality, pollination — nature's invisible infrastructure.

🎨
$10T
Cultural Services

Recreation, spiritual value, ecotourism, aesthetic beauty, educational value — the non-material benefits that enrich human life.

🔬
$21T
Supporting Services

Nutrient cycling, soil formation, primary production, oxygen production — the foundational processes that underpin all other services.

Total: ~$125 Trillion Per Year

Source: Costanza et al., "Changes in the global value of ecosystem services", Global Environmental Change, 2014. This is 1.7× global GDP.

Timeline

Humanity's Environmental Impact Through Time

From early hunter-gatherers to the industrial age, human impact on the environment has accelerated dramatically — especially since 1950.

~300,000 BCE

Early Homo sapiens & Controlled Fire

Early humans used fire to clear vegetation and hunt. Megafauna extinctions began as humans spread across continents — woolly mammoths, giant sloths, and sabre-toothed cats disappeared within millennia of human arrival.

~10,000 BCE

Agricultural Revolution

Farming began in the Fertile Crescent, followed by China, India, and the Americas. Forest clearing for cropland began in earnest. Soil erosion, irrigation-driven salinisation, and overgrazing were humanity's first large-scale environmental impacts.

1750 CE

Industrial Revolution

Coal-powered industrialisation began in Britain. Atmospheric CO₂ started rising above 280 ppm for the first time in 800,000 years. River pollution, urban smog, and industrial deforestation transformed landscapes across Europe and North America.

1950 CE

The Great Acceleration

Population tripled. Plastic production, synthetic fertiliser use, air travel, and consumer economies exploded simultaneously. Scientists now call this the "Great Acceleration" — virtually every indicator of human activity began its steepest upward curve.

1970–2000

Environmental Awakening

The first Earth Day (1970), Clean Air and Water Acts, Montreal Protocol (1987) healing the ozone layer, Rio Earth Summit (1992), Kyoto Protocol (1997). Science and public awareness converged — but emissions continued to rise.

2024 ⚠ Now

Planetary Emergency — and Critical Choice

CO₂ at 422 ppm — the highest in 3 million years. 2023 was the hottest year on record. Yet renewable energy is now the cheapest in history, electric vehicles are mainstream, and international climate agreements are in force. The next decade is the decisive window. The choice is ours.

The Stakes

Why the Environment Matters — To All of Us

Environmental health is not a luxury concern. It underpins every dimension of human wellbeing — from the food on our plates to the stability of our economies and the health of our minds.

❤️

Human Health

WHO attributes 24% of all deaths to environmental factors — air pollution, contaminated water, toxic chemicals, and climate-driven disease spread. Clean air alone could save 7 million lives per year. A healthy environment is public health infrastructure.

💰

Economic Stability

The World Economic Forum rates biodiversity loss and climate change among the top 5 global economic risks. Nature provides $125 trillion/year in services — collapse of even a fraction of this would dwarf all economic crises in history. Green economies outperform fossil-dependent ones.

🌾

Food Security

Stable food production depends on healthy soils, reliable rainfall, functional pollinators, and stable temperatures. Climate change already threatens crop yields in 20+ countries. With 10 billion people to feed by 2050, protecting natural systems is inseparable from feeding the world.

🧘

Mental Health

Over 100+ studies link time in nature to reduced stress, anxiety, and depression. "Blue-green prescribing" is now used in the UK NHS. Eco-anxiety affects 68% of adults in developed countries as environmental news worsens — itself a mental health emergency requiring action.

🎭

Cultural Identity

For 6,000+ Indigenous communities, nature is not just an economic resource but the foundation of spiritual life, language, and cultural identity. As ecosystems disappear, languages, traditions, and ways of knowing vanish with them — irreplaceable losses to human civilisation.

⚖️

Intergenerational Justice

The decisions made in the next 10 years will determine the planet that billions of future generations inherit. A child born today will live through consequences of today's choices — it is a profound ethical obligation to protect the natural systems that make human life possible.
