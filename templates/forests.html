Carbon Science

How Forests Absorb Carbon

Through photosynthesis, trees convert atmospheric CO₂ into woody biomass, sequestering carbon in trunks, roots, and surrounding soil. Old-growth forests are particularly powerful — their deep soils store massive quantities of organic carbon accumulated over millennia.

Forest soils globally store approximately 1 trillion tonnes of carbon — more than in all above-ground biomass combined. Disturbing these soils through logging or agriculture releases this carbon rapidly, creating a massive carbon debt that takes 200+ years to repay.

🌳 The 1 Trillion Trees Initiative
A science-backed plan to restore 900M ha of forest globally — enough to store 205B tonnes of CO₂, equivalent to 2/3 of all human-emitted CO₂ since the Industrial Revolution. Led by UNEP, WEF, and 70+ partner nations.

Cultural Stewardship

Indigenous Communities as Forest Guardians

Indigenous peoples manage just 22% of the world's land — yet that land holds 80% of remaining primary forests and 80% of the world's biodiversity. Their stewardship cost is a fraction of conventional conservation.
